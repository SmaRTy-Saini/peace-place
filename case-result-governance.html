Laboratory reporting

Client-Specific Result Governance in a Shared Workflow

A laboratory needed a specific result element withheld for one client while every other client continued receiving the existing report and electronic payload behavior.

The problem

A laboratory needed a specific result element withheld for one client while every other client continued receiving the existing report and electronic payload behavior.

What I built

  • Scoped the rule to the intended client and effective date.
  • Placed suppression before both human-readable report generation and outbound electronic delivery.
  • Avoided global test-level suppression that could affect unrelated clients.
  • Validated new accessions against the new behavior while preserving historical output.
  • Tested both report and interface paths to ensure the rule was applied consistently.
Outcome

The client-specific requirement was met without introducing side effects into shared production workflows.

Why this work matters

Shared laboratory systems require surgical change control. A technically easy rule can become operationally dangerous if its scope, timing, or downstream delivery paths are not understood.

How I approach similar projects

I start with the real production path: what enters the system, what decisions happen along the way, what should be observable, what can fail, and what the operator needs when something goes wrong. The implementation is then designed around reliability, maintainability, security, and support—not just getting one successful test through.
