What “managed” should actually mean

A managed IT relationship should remove ownership gaps. That includes the obvious user tickets, but also the less visible work: documentation, access control, backup testing, patching, network health, account lifecycle, vendor coordination, recovery planning and keeping recurring issues from returning.

Tier 1, Tier 2 and Tier 3 under one technical owner

Tier 1 covers user-facing issues. Tier 2 moves into systems, networks, identity and infrastructure. Tier 3 covers the deeper engineering work—interfaces, APIs, databases, automation, security and production troubleshooting. The benefit is continuity: the problem does not have to be re-explained every time it crosses a boundary.

Fully managed or co-managed

I can act as the primary IT owner for a small or mid-sized business, or as the senior technical layer behind an internal team that already handles day-to-day support. Co-managed work is especially useful when an internal team needs project capacity, integration knowledge, security hardening or a second set of eyes on infrastructure.

Designed for businesses that have outgrown break-fix

If the owner, office manager or one technical employee is still coordinating every vendor and remembering every password, the business has usually outgrown ad-hoc IT. The first step is to document the environment, identify risk, establish ownership and build a support model that can scale.
